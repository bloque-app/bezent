bezent — Design overview

This document states how the problem is understood and what the specification now says, in plain language, for the owner and a reviewer: the problem, the shape, the Kreivo contract, the reserves, how value flows, the trust it asks for, and what is ruled and what comes next.

SPEC.md names no network or product. This overview does, because a discussion needs them; the names and figures come from PLAN.md. The table below maps one vocabulary onto the other. Identifiers in code point back to SPEC.md or PLAN.md; every sentence says what they require first, so neither document is needed to follow it.

Terms used below

Term Meaning
Finality The point after which a network can no longer revise what it showed, by its own finality signal. Being included in a block comes earlier and is not final
Coordinate What names an on-chain event exactly: network, block number, block hash and position in the block. A transaction hash alone is not one
Arrival Value bezent observed coming into a reserve address. It is evidence that value came in, not yet an instruction to mint
Unassociated arrival An arrival no deposit has claimed yet, or one that can never become a deposit (a token not admitted, an amount outside the route's limits). It waits with a named owner and is never minted, kept or dropped
Disposition What is known about one leg of a transfer: executed, provably not executed, or unknown. bezent derives it only from what it observed itself
Proof of non-execution Final public chain data showing that a signed transaction can never execute: on Kreivo, an expiring transaction whose window has ended; on an EVM chain, its nonce (the sender's transaction counter) used up by a different transaction. Anyone can check it
Attempt One signed transaction that tries to deliver a transfer. If it is proven never to execute, a new, numbered attempt may follow
Assurance class What a backend's facts rest on. Public: anyone can check arrivals, the reserve and proofs from public chain data. Attested: they come from a named party's reports, here Swaps'
Named condition A reason a transfer is held that clears by itself or with evidence, such as a short reserve. It sits on top of a state and is never a failure
On-chain reference A fixed digest of bezent's own identifier for a movement, written on chain with it, so each movement is matched exactly once and never by amount or time
Supply ceiling, mint window, per-transfer maximum Limits the Kreivo contract enforces by its own code: how much of an asset may exist, how much may be minted in each 24-hour window, and the largest single mint
Refund mint A mint that makes a redeemer whole when every payout attempt of their redemption is proven never to execute. One per redemption, counted against the mint window
Commitment A hash of a redemption's payout instructions. For fiat, only the hash goes on chain; bezent accepts the bank details later only if they hash to it
Surplus Bloque's own value held in a reserve beyond what supply needs. It absorbs every cost and shortfall, may never go negative, and is kept above a floor Bloque declares
Reserve book bezent's own record of what backs each asset: double-entry, append-only, and never the authority (§5.1)
Statement A signed claim, published daily per asset, of every term of the reserve equation (§5), naming the final blocks it rests on. A claim, not a proof of solvency

What changed since edition 4 (2026-10-11)

  • A narrower job. bezent now moves value only between each asset's reserve and Kreivo. It issues no deposit addresses and sweeps nothing: evmhot sweeps into the vault, Swaps routes value in, and Swaps claims each arrival by pointing at its coordinate (§1.6, §2, §6.1).
  • Conversions are Swaps', inside the vault. Swaps holds a role on the Polygon vault that can only turn USDT0 into USDC, with the output landing back in the vault. bezent mints what that conversion delivered (§6.4).
  • Payouts only on the reserve's own network. A bUSDC holder who wants USDC on Base is paid on Polygon to Swaps, which bridges it with CCTP from its own funds (§3, §6.2).
  • A fiat payment reversed after Swaps reported it final is Swaps' to cover, within one business day. Holders are never clawed back, and mints pause while surplus is below its floor (§2.1, §6.3).
  • Keys, book and precision. The keys that move value sign inside AWS KMS (§8). bezent keeps its own reserve book, which is never the authority (§5.1). bBTC keeps bitcoin's 8 decimals, and the sub-satoshi remainder of a BTCB deposit goes to surplus (§3).
  • The contract and the lifecycle, tightened. A redemption is identified by its redeemer together with the reference it chose (§4). Which state may follow which is now normative, with ways out of review and numbered retry attempts that keep their reference (§7.1). Three gaps in the request surface are closed (§7.2).
  • Vault owners are limited, and the first bounds are set. A Safe guard stops the owners from moving the vault's tokens, and widening changes wait out a delay (§8). Starting limits exist for bUSDC and COPM (§4.1).
  • Nothing is open on the decision form. Every open question in the spec is closed (§10).

1. The problem, as understood

1.1 What is needed

Bloque's money is being rebuilt around one book on one network. aledgent keeps the book, and Kreivo is its only settlement network. The ramp was ruled on 2026-09-29 to hold no minting rights.

But the value people and partners actually hold lives elsewhere: USDC and USDT on Polygon and Base, EURC on Base, BTC tokens, and pesos and reais in bank accounts. Something has to make that value exist on Kreivo one to one, pay it back out of the reserve when someone redeems, keep a reserve that anyone can compare with supply, and observe every outcome, never assume it. That something is bezent.

1.2 What happens today

There is no bridge in that sense. There is a chain of third parties, run by Swaps' graphs:

Route Path today
USDC on Polygon/Base → Kreivo Sweep to Brale → DUSD on Asset Hub → a batched XCM transfer to Kreivo → a transfer to the user
USDT on Polygon/Base → Kreivo Sweep to Binance → convert → withdraw USDC on Solana to Brale → DUSD → XCM → transfer
Kreivo → USDC on a user's address No registered route

Every arrow is a leg on someone else's system, glued together with lanes, batches, a cron and callbacks.

1.3 Why it fails

notes/BRIDGE-REQUIREMENTS.md §5 lists sixteen incidents in six classes. Each is a requirement now:

Class What happened What the spec requires
"Failed", then paid Ten cash-outs succeeded after a failure signal; 443.44 USD absorbed Every leg is executed, provably not executed, or unknown. A refund needs a final proof that the leg did not execute: never a timer, never an unknown (REQ-NX-*, REQ-RF-1)
Non-execution never provable No expiry on any transaction; a nonce released on a timer broke another operation Every signed transaction can be proven not executed in bounded time: on Kreivo it expires; on EVM another final transaction uses up its nonce (REQ-NX-1)
Re-signing on silence A quiet submission was signed again: double execution Store the exact signed bytes before sending. After a restart, re-send them or check their status. A second attempt keeps the first one's on-chain reference and is signed only once the first is proven never to execute (REQ-EO-1, REQ-EO-6)
"Could not look" read as "did not happen" An RPC rotation quarantined a successful transfer A timeout, a lost connection, a node's "not found" or an event that cannot be read is unknown, never failed. During an incident, transfers in flight are reported unknown or delayed and never refunded (REQ-NX-3, REQ-IC-3)
Blocks skipped, clocks rewritten A watcher skipped blocks; a payout booked as failed for 12 h Read every block in order, and never move past a range that failed to read: record it as a gap and fill it. Times are written once, from chain data, and never overwritten (REQ-OB-3, NFR-3)
Limits found too late A withdrawal below an exchange minimum failed after earlier legs ran Each route declares its limits up front (minimum, maximum, window, cut-offs, capacity). What cannot execute is refused before any value moves. A cut-off, a time when the route does not run, holds a transfer until the route reopens and never refuses one (REQ-LM-*)

1.4 What bezent must guarantee

  1. One to one, provably. Every unit of a bridged asset is backed by a unit of its origin token.
  2. Certainty of outcome. Executed, provably not executed, or explicitly unknown. Never guessed.
  3. Exactly once. A deposit is minted once; a withdrawal is paid once; a lost database changes neither.
  4. Payout from the one reserve. Each asset has one reserve and is paid out on that reserve's network. Delivery to any other network is Swaps', from what it receives.
  5. Controlled, but not trusted, by its requester. On a public backend, Swaps cannot make bezent mint value that did not arrive, nor take value out of a vault. On an attested backend, its report is the evidence, bounded on chain.
  6. A known worst case. A stolen key or a defective component has a bounded, stated loss.

1.5 Why trusted, and why now

A message-backed bridge (the Kreivo Bridge spec, 2026-09-19) removes the custodian, but needs infrastructure per network that does not exist yet. Its own first release, R0, is a trusted issuer: Bloque minting against reserves it holds and publishes. This bridge is that R0, built as a system. Bloque is the custodian of every reserve; in return the design is simple and reaches any network where Bloque can hold a token. A later binding can take an asset over with its identity unchanged and without holders having to act (REQ-AS-6).

Asset Hub is out (ruling, 2026-10-01). Asset Hub ⇄ Kreivo already moves over XCM, which is trustless. A custodian on that path would only add trust.

1.6 What bezent is not

Not a router: it issues no deposit addresses, sweeps nothing and moves nothing between networks. evmhot sweeps into the vault, Swaps routes value in, and Swaps carries a payout on to another network. Not an exchange: it never converts and never prices; Swaps converts inside the vault, and bezent mints what the conversion delivered. Not a ledger: it holds reserves, not balances for people. Not a workflow engine: Swaps orchestrates. Not trustless: it states its trust and makes it checkable. Not an eligibility check: it holds no personal data.

2. The shape: one core, many backends

2.1 Assurance classes

Every backend declares its assurance class, public or attested (REQ-BE-2), and the statement names each asset's backend and class (REQ-BE-3).

Public (EVM) Attested (Swaps)
An arrival is final when the chain's own finality signal says so Swaps reports the payment final under its rail's rules (REQ-OB-7)
Who can check the reserve anyone, from the published vault addresses and public chain data (REQ-BK-6) nobody but Swaps. bezent records each report with Swaps' own identifiers, and the statement says the reserve is attested (REQ-BE-4)
Proof of a failed payout the payout's nonce used up by another transaction at a final block, checkable by anyone (REQ-NX-2) Swaps' report
Can the requester cause a mint? No. It only says where value goes Yes. Its report counts as the evidence that value arrived (REQ-BE-5)
A payment undone after it was final Cannot happen: a final chain transfer is not reversed Swaps covers it within one business day (REQ-BE-6)

What "attested" costs, stated plainly. For COPM, MXNB and BRLB, the chain bounds how much can be minted. It cannot tell a real payment from a reported one. What limits a bad report is the contract's mint window and supply ceiling, and the statement, which names Swaps as the party whose word backs the asset.

A payment reversed later (ruled 2026-10-13, REQ-BE-6). A mint always waits for the rail's own finality, since no rail settles instantly. What remains is a reversal after that, such as a chargeback or a fraud recall. Swaps reports it; bezent records the report as an observation; the reserve falls by that amount and the shortfall is charged to the asset's surplus. Swaps must restore the reserve within one business day. Nobody's balance of the bridged asset is reduced, and nothing already minted is taken back. If the shortfall takes the surplus below its floor, new mints of that asset pause until the reserve is restored.

Relying on Swaps (ruling of 2026-10-01) still holds, and Swaps now does more of the work around bezent: it funds the vaults, converts USDT0 inside the Polygon vault, and carries payouts to other networks from its own funds. On a public backend it is still never trusted for an outcome. Its one role on a vault cannot spend the origin token or send anything out of the vault, and it never mints, asserts an EVM arrival, or holds bezent's keys.

3. One reserve per asset

Each bridged asset has exactly one reserve backend, and for a network backend, exactly one network (REQ-AS-8). Tokens of different issuers never back one asset (REQ-AS-3). Every payout is made on the reserve's network (REQ-OT-4).

Asset on Kreivo Backend Class Reserve Decimals Paid out
bUSDC EVM public native USDC in the Polygon vault 6, as USDC on Polygon only
bEURC EVM public EURC in the Base vault 6, as EURC on Base only
bBTC EVM public BTCB in the BNB Chain vault 8, as bitcoin (BTCB has 18) on BNB Chain only
COPM, MXNB, BRLB Swaps attested Swaps' bank accounts, each position itemised 2, as today over Swaps' bank rails
  • Where they live on Kreivo (ruled 2026-10-13). bUSDC is local asset Here(101), bEURC Here(102), bBTC Here(103), and COPM already exists at Here(100). MXNB and BRLB will be created at Here(104) and Here(105) when their attested backend goes live. All are "not sufficient": holding them alone does not keep an account in existence.
  • bBTC is "Binance's BTC". BTCB is a claim on Binance's custody of BTC, so bBTC carries that issuer (REQ-AS-3). BNB Chain is EVM, so no Bitcoin backend is needed.
  • Less precision than the reserve, and the dust rule (REQ-AS-9, REQ-AS-10). One bBTC unit is one satoshi (a hundred-millionth of a bitcoin); one BTCB unit is 10⁻¹⁸ of a bitcoin, so every bBTC unit is 10¹⁰ BTCB units. A BTCB deposit mints the arrival divided by 10¹⁰, rounded down. The remainder, always less than one satoshi, is recorded as surplus with the deposit's reference: it is never minted, dropped or returned. A payout pays exactly the redeemed amount times 10¹⁰, so it never leaves a remainder. A depositor therefore gives up less than one satoshi per deposit, and each route's minimum is at least one unit of the bridged asset.
  • One reserve means nothing to rebalance. If the reserve cannot cover a withdrawal, it waits under a named condition (CND-ReserveShort): it never fails, is never refunded and never moves to another network, and it resumes by itself when the reserve recovers (REQ-OT-5).
  • Another network is Swaps' job (ruled 2026-10-13). A bUSDC holder who wants USDC on Base redeems with Swaps' own Polygon account as the beneficiary. bezent pays Swaps on Polygon, and Swaps bridges the USDC to Base with CCTP from its own funds. bezent observes only the Polygon payout. Why: a role on the vault could not tell a payout a redemption funded from one no redemption funded, so every outflow stays one kind, signed by bezent. Cost: a Base user's withdrawal takes one more hop, and Swaps carries the CCTP float.

4. The Kreivo contract

The Kreivo end is one ink! contract, alone in a dedicated ContractsStore community. kreivo#512 lets a contract mint and burn a local asset when its community account is the asset's Issuer and Admin. The community is dedicated, so no other contract and no other holder can use that authority. Its governance is the upgrade authority: an upgrade passes that published process, with a delay before it takes effect (REQ-HN-10, REQ-GV-3).

One contract serves every bridged asset, each with its own bounds (ruled 2026-10-13). That means one community, one deployment and one governance. It also means a defect in the contract, or a stolen delivery key, reaches every asset at once; each asset's own bounds cap what it can lose.

Operation Who may call What it does Refuses when
Mint The delivery role Mints to the address named in the order, with an on-chain reference That mint reference was used before; above the per-transfer maximum, the mint window or the ceiling; paused
Redeem Anyone Takes the asset from the caller and burns it in the same call; records the backend, the payout instructions or their commitment, and a reference the redeemer chooses Paused; below the route's minimum; instructions do not fit the backend; this redeemer has used this reference before
Refund mint The delivery role Mints once to the redeemer of a redemption whose every payout attempt is proven never to execute That redemption was refunded before; the mint window would be exceeded
Pause, set bounds The pause role or governance; only governance raises a bound Stops new work; sets ceiling, window, per-transfer maximum A raise without the governance delay
  • Mint goes where the order says: an address on Kreivo (REQ-IN-5). For aledgent, that is Swaps' Kreivo account; Swaps then deposits into aledgent. bezent never calls aledgent, holds its keys or reports to it (REQ-LG-1).
  • Recipients are responsible for receiving. bezent does not check whether a destination can receive a "not sufficient" asset. If Kreivo refuses a mint because of its destination, nothing is minted and the deposit waits under a named condition (CND-DestinationRefused), then goes to review past its deadline (REQ-IN-9).
  • Redeem is permissionless, so any Kreivo account or DAO can exit. kreivo#512's burn can take from any account, so the contract pulls the amount into itself first and burns only what it holds (REQ-HN-8).
  • A redemption is identified by its redeemer and its reference together (amendment 0008). The contract's review found a hole in a single shared set of references: a mint's reference can be computed from public data, so anyone could redeem the minimum using it first and block that mint forever. Now mint references and redemption references are kept apart, and a redemption is named by (redeemer, reference). A payout is bound to its redemption by that pair, never by amount or time (REQ-OT-2, REQ-ID-2).
  • Payout instructions (REQ-RQ-9). For EVM, the destination address goes inline: it is public on the destination chain anyway. For fiat, only the commitment (a hash) goes on chain. Swaps relays the bank details later, and bezent accepts them only if they hash to it. No personal data goes on any network (REQ-PV-1).
  • Burnt at once, refunded by mint. A failed payout is made whole by the mint authority: one refund mint per redemption, within the mint window, and only once every payout attempt is proven never to execute (REQ-OT-6, REQ-RF-4, INV-8). bezent may instead try the payout again until the deadline (REQ-RF-5).

notes/GENERALISATION.md §9 proposed escrow until the payout is final. The owner chose to burn at once (2026-10-11), and SPEC.md follows that.

4.1 Starting bounds

The spec says each asset's limits are sized from Swaps' current volumes and recorded in the plan. The owner ruled the first values on 2026-10-13 (DEC-17), from a measurement of Swaps' production volumes:

Asset Supply ceiling Mint window (24 h) Largest single mint
bUSDC (in USD) 360,000 120,000 50,000
COPM (in pesos) 90,000,000 30,000,000 16,000,000

The volume bezent must sustain without redesign is ten times what Swaps does today (NFR-10); the figures are kept privately. The other assets had no orders in that period, so bEURC and bBTC take the bUSDC figures, and MXNB and BRLB the COPM figures, converted at the day's exchange rate when their bounds are configured; the rate and its source are recorded with each configuration.

5. Reserves and the reserve equation

For each bridged asset, at a final point on every network (SPEC.md §5.3):

Σ Reserve + InTransit = Supply + OwedMint + OwedPayout + Surplus
Term Meaning
Reserve What bezent holds of the origin token at the asset's one reserve address: the vault, or Swaps' itemised accounts for fiat
InTransit Value bezent has sent through a protocol venue that has not yet been delivered. In V0 it is always zero: bezent sends nothing through a venue, since Swaps converts inside the vault and carries cross-network payouts itself. The term stays for later
Supply The asset in existence on Kreivo
OwedMint Final arrivals of the origin token not yet minted, whether a deposit request has claimed them yet or not
OwedPayout Final redemptions whose payout is not yet final. The holder's asset is already burnt; the reserve still owes them
Surplus Bloque's own value in the reserve. Never negative. Absorbs every cost and shortfall

A worked example, for bUSDC. The Polygon vault holds 1,000 USDC. Supply on Kreivo is 940. A 30 USDC arrival is final but not yet minted. Two redemptions are final and unpaid: a 15 payout signed but not yet final, and a 5 waiting its turn. Then:

1,000 + 0 = 940 + 30 + (15 + 5) + 10

Surplus is 10. When the 30 is minted, supply becomes 970 and OwedMint 0. When the 15 payout is final, the vault holds 985 and OwedPayout drops to 5. If the 5 payout is proven never to execute, its refund mint moves 5 from OwedPayout back to supply. Costs and shortfalls fall on surplus: a COPM payment reversed after Swaps reported it final lowers both the reserve and the surplus until Swaps restores it; a bBTC deposit's sub-satoshi remainder raises surplus. A delivery always equals the transfer's amount and is never reduced (REQ-BK-4, REQ-TR-5).

The equation is checked at least once a minute, at zero tolerance; any divergence, to the smallest unit, pauses the asset's routes and pages a human (REQ-BK-1, NFR-5). OwedMint and OwedPayout are each a list of the transfers that make them up, never a figure left over from a subtraction (REQ-BK-2). Every reserve address is published (REQ-BK-5). Unconverted USDT0 sits outside the equation and backs nothing; it is itemised and published in the statement (REQ-CV-4).

The statement is published at least daily per asset, signed by a key used for nothing else, and kept in a public, append-only bucket with object lock, so a later revision is detectable without bezent's help. It names every term with the final blocks it rests on, each asset's backend and assurance class, and which terms anyone can recompute from public data and which rest on Swaps' reports. It says it is a signed claim, not a proof of solvency (REQ-PR-1, REQ-PR-2, REQ-BE-3).

5.1 The reserve book

bezent keeps its own reserve book per asset (amendment 0003): a double-entry, append-only record of every movement into, out of and between the reserve, the owed amounts and surplus, and of supply as Kreivo reports it. A correction is a new entry; nothing is ever changed or deleted (REQ-BK-7, INV-17). It lives in bezent's own database, whose triggers refuse any change or deletion; there is no separate aledgent deployment for it.

The book is working state, never the authority. Each minute's check re-derives every term from the networks and Swaps' reports at final points and compares it with the book; any difference is a divergence (REQ-BK-8). The book itself is not provable to outsiders, and the statement says so; what outsiders can check is the chain data and, for fiat, Swaps' reports (REQ-BK-9).

5.2 The surplus floor

Bloque declares a floor for each asset's surplus, an alert fires before the surplus reaches it, and Bloque tops it up from its own funds (REQ-BK-10). For an attested asset, a reversal that takes the surplus below the floor also pauses new mints of that asset until Swaps restores the reserve (§2.1).

6. How value flows

6.1 Deposit: USDC on Polygon → bUSDC on Kreivo

sequenceDiagram
    participant F as Funder (Swaps or an evmhot sweep)
    participant V as Polygon vault
    participant B as bezent
    participant S as Swaps
    participant K as Bridge contract (Kreivo)
    F->>V: transfer 100 USDC
    B->>B: observe the arrival, wait for Polygon finality
    B->>B: hold it as an unassociated arrival
    S->>B: deposit request naming the arrival's coordinate and a destination
    B->>B: check the coordinate itself, the deposit starts at Source-Final
    B->>K: mint 100 bUSDC to Swaps' Kreivo account
    K->>K: check reference, bounds, pause
    B->>B: observe the mint at Kreivo finality
    B-->>S: Completed
    S->>S: deposit into aledgent
  • Nothing is minted before the arrival is final (INV-6). On Polygon that takes seconds.
  • Value comes in by a plain transfer into the vault. bezent issues no deposit addresses and sweeps nothing. evmhot is being revamped to sweep its deposit addresses straight into the vault, and Swaps may also transfer in directly. bezent sees only arrivals at the vault.
  • Swaps claims an arrival by pointing at it. Its deposit request names the arrival's coordinate, a destination on Kreivo and a refund destination (REQ-RQ-3). bezent checks that coordinate itself, at finality; it is consumed once, so one arrival backs one deposit (INV-2). Nothing is matched by amount or time, and there are no standing instructions any more.
  • Stray value is never lost and never kept. An arrival that nobody claims by the route's deadline, or that can never become a deposit (a token not admitted, an amount outside the route's limits), stays an unassociated arrival (CND-Unassociated) owned by Bloque's operations function, which acts within one business day. It is never minted, absorbed into reserve or dropped; releasing it needs a second approver (REQ-IN-6, REQ-RV-1).

    H->>K: redeem 100 bUSDC with reference r, pay to a Polygon address
    K->>K: take and burn 100, record (holder, r)
    B->>B: observe the redemption at Kreivo finality
    B->>B: sign attempt 1 and store its bytes first
    B->>V: payout of 100 USDC with its on-chain reference
    V->>R: 100 USDC
    B->>B: observe the payout at Polygon finality
    B->>B: Completed, in the sequenced log
  • Nothing is paid before the redemption is final (INV-7). The payout is bound to the redemption by the pair (redeemer, reference), never by amount or time (REQ-OT-2).
  • Value and reference move together. The vault executes, in one transaction, the USDC transfer and a call to a tiny contract whose only job is to emit the reference, so the payout and its reference are atomic. The delivery role may do exactly that, within its allowance, and nothing else (DEC-10).
  • To another network. A holder who wants USDC on Base names Swaps' Polygon account as the beneficiary; Swaps then bridges with CCTP from its own funds (§3). bezent's part ends at the Polygon payout.
  • If the payout is proven never to execute, the holder gets one refund mint, or bezent makes a new attempt until the withdrawal's deadline. Each attempt is numbered, keeps the same on-chain reference, and is signed only once the previous one is proven never to execute (REQ-EO-6). A refund never happens on a timer or on an unknown outcome; a payout still unknown past its deadline goes to review, owned by Bloque's operations within one business day (REQ-RF-1, REQ-RF-5, REQ-RV-1).

6.3 Deposit: pesos → COPM on Kreivo, attested by Swaps

sequenceDiagram
    participant U as Payer
    participant S as Swaps
    participant B as bezent
    participant K as Bridge contract (Kreivo)
    U->>S: pay 50,000 COP (Bre-B or PSE)
    S->>S: wait until the rail makes it final
    S->>B: report arrival final, with Swaps' id
    S->>B: deposit request, with destination
    B->>B: record the report as the observation

    B-->>S: Completed
  • Swaps' report is the evidence (REQ-BE-5), recorded with Swaps' own identifiers (REQ-BE-4). Only a requester holding the attesting grant may report. The contract's mint window and supply ceiling limit a wrong report.
  • Swaps must not report a payment final before its rail guarantees it (REQ-OB-7). The owner's note: whether by Bre-B, PSE, a payment link or a bank deposit, the payment must clear before issuance is ordered.
  • A reversal after that (a chargeback, a fraud recall) is Swaps' to cover within one business day, charged meanwhile to surplus, with holders never clawed back and mints paused under the surplus floor (§2.1).
  • Redeeming COPM works like §6.2, with a hash on chain instead of an address. Swaps relays the bank details, bezent checks them against the hash and refuses them if they do not match, and Swaps pays out on its rail and reports the payout.

6.4 USDT0 in, converted by Swaps

USDT0 on Polygon is the one convertible token, admitted only for bUSDC (ruled 2026-10-13). bEURC and bBTC have none, so the Base and BNB Chain vaults carry no conversion role.

sequenceDiagram
    participant F as Funder
    participant V as Polygon vault
    participant S as Swaps

    participant B as bezent

    F->>V: 100 USDT0
    B->>B: observe at finality, hold it as awaiting conversion (not reserve)

  • USDT0 is never reserve. Until converted it backs nothing; it waits under a named condition (CND-AwaitingConversion), itemised outside the equation and listed in the statement (REQ-CV-1, REQ-CV-4).
  • Swaps converts, inside the vault (REQ-CV-7). Its role allows one thing: an exact-input swap through Uniswap v3's router on the pinned 0.01 % USDT0/USDC pool, with the vault as recipient, within an allowance per window. Zodiac Roles enforces on chain that the role spends only USDT0, never USDC, and sends the output only to the same vault. It cannot move value out by any other path.
  • bezent mints what the swap delivered, at its observed amount, and the deposit records both amounts (REQ-CV-2). Price, slippage and minimum output are Swaps'; bezent never mints up to a quoted amount (REQ-CV-5).

  • A conversion not done by its deadline goes to review; its USDT0 stays itemised, never written off and never counted as reserve (REQ-CV-6).

7. The lifecycle of a transfer

7.1 States, and which may follow which

State Meaning Terminal
Received → Accepted → Source-Final Taken in → within route rules → source final and consumed for this transfer
Delivering → Delivered A destination transaction is signed and stored → it is included, not yet final
Completed Destination final and executed in full Yes
Refunding → Refunded Destination proven not executed; the refund runs → the refund is complete Yes (Refunded)
Refused, Expired, Withdrawn Refused at request; source never arrived in time; or the source stopped matching the chain before finality. Nothing moved Yes
Needs-Review A leg is still unknown past its deadline, an execution was partial or of the wrong amount, or a condition that blocks delivery is past its deadline. A named owner resolves it within one business day. Never refunded, retried or failed by default

The spec now fixes which state may follow which (amendment 0009, REQ-TR-6). A transfer moves only along these edges:

stateDiagram-v2
    direction LR
    state "Source-Final" as SF
    state "Needs-Review" as NR
    [*] --> Received
    [*] --> SF: deposit claiming a final arrival
    Received --> Refused
    Received --> Accepted
    Accepted --> SF
    Accepted --> Expired
    Accepted --> Withdrawn
    Accepted --> NR
    SF --> Delivering
    SF --> NR
    Delivering --> Delivered
    Delivering --> Delivering: new attempt
    Delivered --> Delivering: new attempt
    Delivering --> Refunding
    Delivered --> Refunding
    Delivering --> NR
    Delivered --> NR
    Delivered --> Completed
    Refunding --> Refunded
    NR --> SF: evidence
    NR --> Delivering: evidence
    NR --> Completed: evidence
  • A deposit that claims a final arrival starts at Source-Final, because its source is already final; nothing else skips straight there.
  • A new attempt loops back to Delivering only after the previous attempt is proven never to execute. Each attempt is numbered and recorded and keeps the same on-chain reference, since a reference is used up only by a leg that actually executed (REQ-EO-6).
  • Leaving review takes evidence. A transfer leaves Needs-Review only by one ordinary step from the state it held before review, and only on a coordinate that settles what happened, which bezent verifies itself. A resolution that moves value needs a second approver (REQ-RV-2, REQ-GV-5). The diagram shows the usual exits; the rule allows any one ordinary step.
  • A refund has its own lifecycle and review, so Refunding never goes to Needs-Review itself.
  • A terminal state never changes (INV-5).

Named conditions sit on top of a state and clear by themselves or by evidence. None is a failure. The ones that matter most here (SPEC.md §10):

  • CND-Unassociated: a final arrival nobody has claimed yet, or one that can never become a deposit; it has an owner.
  • CND-ReserveShort: the asset's reserve cannot yet cover a withdrawal.
  • CND-MintWindow: the mint window is spent; minting resumes when the window rolls over.
  • CND-DestinationRefused: Kreivo refused a mint because of its destination; nothing was minted.
  • CND-AwaitingConversion: a USDT0 arrival waiting for Swaps' conversion; it is never reserve.
  • CND-CutOff: the transfer's next step falls outside a time its route does not run; it resumes when the route reopens.
  • CND-FeeShort: an address lacks the network's native token to pay for its next transaction.
  • CND-Paused: new work on this route waits; work in flight continues.

Included, final and completed are kept apart, each with its own time taken from chain data (REQ-TR-4). Latency uses only those write-once times (NFR-3), against each route's published completion budget: a p99 target and ceiling, measured from the source leg's final block to the destination leg's, first measured on a test network that reproduces the route and then published (NFR-2).

7.2 The request surface

Swaps talks to bezent over HTTP with JSON, using scoped service credentials. Each kind of request needs its own grant (REQ-PN-2):

  • Request a deposit: the coordinate of a final arrival at a reserve, a destination on Kreivo and a refund destination (REQ-RQ-3).
  • Add a redemption's extras: a refund destination, an expiry and, for fiat, the relayed bank details. The redemption itself is the request; the extras are attached to it by its reference (REQ-RQ-4).
  • Request a refund of an unassociated arrival: its coordinate and a destination, with a second approver at Bloque (REQ-RQ-6).
  • Attest, for fiat: Swaps' reports of arrivals, payouts, balances and reversals (REQ-BE-5).

Every request carries the requester's own reference, scoped to that requester. Repeating a request with the same body returns the original's status; the same reference with a different body is refused. Nothing a request says about a chain is believed: it is a pointer bezent checks itself (REQ-RQ-7). Every state change and condition is appended to a gapless, numbered log that Swaps can read from its last position, so a lost notification is never fatal; notifications are signed webhooks delivered at least once (REQ-ST-4, REQ-ST-5).

Building the ingress found three gaps, closed on 2026-10-13 (amendment 0010):

  • Extras are written once. For fiat, only the requester holding the attesting grant may add them. For a chain asset, any requester holding the withdrawal grant may, and the first accepted wins; a later, different version is refused.
  • Cut-offs hold, never refuse. Refusing makes no sense for a deposit whose value is already in the reserve, so a transfer that meets a cut-off waits under CND-CutOff and resumes by itself.
  • Two refusals now have names: a request that does not parse or lacks a required field (ERR-Malformed), and relayed bank details that do not hash to the redemption's commitment (ERR-CommitmentMismatch).

8. Trust, stated plainly

Question Answer
Can Swaps make bezent mint bUSDC, bEURC or bBTC that did not arrive? No. A mint needs a final, unconsumed arrival bezent observed itself, and the contract refuses a reused reference (INV-2, INV-6).
Can Swaps make bezent mint COPM, MXNB or BRLB that did not arrive? Yes, up to the mint window and the ceiling. For these, Swaps' word is the observation. The statement names Swaps as the party it rests on (REQ-BE-5).
Can Swaps take value out of a vault? No. Its only role, on the Polygon vault, swaps USDT0 into USDC that lands back in the same vault, within an allowance. On-chain code stops it from spending USDC or sending anything elsewhere (REQ-CV-7).
Can Swaps send someone's deposit to the wrong place? Yes. Where a deposit goes is Swaps' decision. It can misdirect value; on a public backend it cannot create it.
Who covers a fiat payment reversed after Swaps reported it final? Swaps, within one business day. Surplus absorbs it meanwhile; holders keep what they hold; mints pause if surplus falls below its floor (REQ-BE-6).
Can Bloque mint unbacked supply? Yes, up to the contract's bounds. Kreivo cannot see Polygon. The ceiling and window cap it; the reserve equation shows it to anyone who checks. Until the asset referendum moves bUSDC, bEURC and bBTC to the dedicated community, a Bloque account still holds their Issuer and Admin roles; any supply it created would show as a divergence and pause the asset.
What can a stolen delivery key do? Mint up to the window on Kreivo, refund mints included: one per redemption, so refunds cannot exceed the window. Pay out up to the vault's delivery allowance per period on an EVM chain (REQ-KY-3). Delivery keys sign inside AWS KMS and never leave it, so "stolen" means someone gained the right to ask KMS to sign, not a copy of the key (REQ-CU-1).
Can the vault's owners move the reserve? Not directly. A Safe guard refuses any owner transaction that transfers or approves the vault's tokens. Widening changes (a new role or member, a higher allowance, a new module, new owners) take effect only after a delay; narrowing ones are immediate, like a pause. No vault is funded before both are in place.
Can value be lost at a venue? Not reserve. bezent sends no value through a venue. Swaps' conversions spend only USDT0, which backs nothing, and their output lands in the same vault.
Who is the custodian? Bloque, on every backend. A bridged asset is a claim on Bloque's reserves; for fiat, they sit in Swaps' accounts.
Can governance move reserves? Through upgrade, yes. On Kreivo that is the dedicated community's governance, with a published delay (REQ-HN-10, REQ-GV-3); on an EVM network, it is the vault owners' widening changes, each behind the delay.
What can nobody check? That Bloque will honour a withdrawal; for attested assets, that a reported payment was real; and the reserve book itself, which the statement says is not provable.

9. Scope

V0: one issuing network; one or more backends; each asset on one reserve backend; protocol venues only; no fast path (nothing is minted or delivered before its source is final). It is proven first on a test network that reproduces Kreivo, before any value: zombienet with Kreivo 0.17.1, plus Polygon Amoy, Base Sepolia, BNB Chain testnet and local forks of each.

V0 exits when: the reserve equation holds at exactly zero divergence, sustained; every final source leg is delivered, refunded or held in a named condition with an owner; every runbook has been exercised.

Someone else's work, around bezent:

  • Deposit addresses and sweeps: evmhot.
  • Getting value into a reserve, from any network: Swaps, later rutent.
  • Converting other tokens into the origin token: Swaps, inside the vault, under its role.
  • Delivering to a network other than the reserve's: Swaps, from what it receives.

Deferred:

  • A fast path (DEF-1): minting or delivering before the source is final, from Bloque's own inventory, with a capped exposure that is Bloque's loss if the source reverts. Ruled out for V0 on 2026-10-13.
  • Several attesters per mint (DEF-2): k of n independent parties confirming an arrival before the contract mints, so no single key is trusted.
  • Bridge fees (DEF-4): charging requesters. Pricing is the requester's; bezent charges nothing.
  • Automatic return of stray deposits (DEF-5): the sender is often an exchange's shared address, where returned value can be lost.
  • Custodial venues (DEF-8): an exchange such as Binance, for conversion.

Tokens of a different precision were deferred until 2026-10-13; they are now in scope, with the dust rule of §3.

Out for good: Asset Hub, or any network with a trustless channel to Kreivo; pricing; holding balances for people; eligibility.
