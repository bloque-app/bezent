bezent Spec

A trusted, mirrored bridge. Each bridged asset has one reserve, held by the operator on one backend, and each backend declares whether its facts are public or attested. Value deposited there is minted, one to one, on one issuing network, to the destination the order names. Anyone may redeem a bridged asset. It is destroyed at once, and the origin token is paid out from the reserve, on its network. A payout proven never to execute is made whole by one refund mint. bezent never calls a ledger's contract.

Networks and backends

bezent connects exactly one issuing network, where it mints and destroys bridged assets, with backends. A backend is the counterpart system for one class of origin: a network, or a financial rail reached through a named party. Each backend declares an assurance class (REQ-BE-2). A network backend is an origin network, where bezent holds the tokens that back a bridged asset. This document defines the issuing network and origin networks only by the requirements they must satisfy (§4.4). In this text, "the issuing network" and "an origin network" mean whatever satisfies those requirements, never a particular network.

1. Purpose and scope

1.1 The problem

The domain. Value that people and businesses want to use is issued on many networks. A stable token issued on one network can be held, but not spent, on another. A system whose book of balances settles on one network needs that value there, and needs to send it back out to whichever network the holder names.

What a bridge between them must give.

  1. One to one, provably. Every unit of a bridged asset is backed by one unit of its origin token, held by bezent, at every point anyone checks. Anyone can recompute the backing from public data.
  2. Certainty of outcome. Every movement bezent makes is executed, provably not executed, or explicitly unresolved. It is never guessed. "I could not look" is never "it did not happen".
  3. Exactly once. A deposit is minted once. A withdrawal is delivered once. Retries, restarts and lost databases change neither.
  4. Delivery where it is asked for. bezent keeps one reserve per asset and pays out on the reserve's network, to the beneficiary the redemption names. Delivery to any other network is the requester's, from what it receives.
  5. Controlled, but not trusted, by its requester. The system that asks for transfers decides what moves and to whom. On a public backend it cannot make bezent mint value that did not arrive. On an attested backend its report is the evidence, and the mint window and supply ceiling bound it (REQ-BE-5).
  6. A known worst case. A stolen key, a defective component or a failed exchange has a bounded, stated maximum loss.

The problem. Moving value between networks through a chain of third parties (an exchange, an issuer's custody, an intermediate network) turns one movement into many legs. Each leg is a place where an outcome is assumed instead of observed, and where a timeout is read as a failure. Bloque's experience with such paths is recorded in notes/BRIDGE-REQUIREMENTS.md §5. A message-backed bridge removes the custodian, but it needs infrastructure that does not exist yet for every network.

bezent, as specified here, is the other option: a trusted bridge. The operator is the custodian of every reserve. In return, it is simple, it reaches any network on which the operator can hold a token, and it states its trust plainly (§4.3) and makes it checkable (§4.6).

1.2 What bezent is

bezent is a service with one on-chain component on the issuing network. It moves value between each bridged asset's reserve and the issuing network: arrivals at a reserve, mints, redemptions, and payouts from a reserve. Getting value into a reserve (deposit addresses, sweeps, routing between networks) and converting other tokens into the origin token are the requester's. It provides:

  • Arrivals at a reserve. bezent observes value that arrives at a reserve address, whoever sent it.
  • Deposits. When an arrival is final on its backend and a requester binds it with a deposit request, bezent mints the same amount of the bridged asset on the issuing network, to the destination named in the order.
  • Withdrawals. Anyone holding a bridged asset may redeem it on the issuing network. The redemption destroys it at once and records where the backend pays it out. bezent pays the origin token from the asset's reserve. If every payout attempt is proven never to execute, the redeemer gets one refund mint.
  • One reserve per asset. Each bridged asset has one reserve, on one backend. Deposits fill it and withdrawals draw on it. Every payout is made on the reserve's network; delivery to another network is the requester's.
  • Backends with an assurance class. Each backend is public, when its facts can be checked from public data, or attested, when they rest on a named party's reports.
  • Proof of reserves. bezent publishes every address that holds reserve. Anyone can compare reserves with supply.
  • Outcomes, not verdicts. Every report bezent makes is an observation at a coordinate, with finality as observed.

bezent does not know who a funder is, what product a holder uses, or what anything costs. It sees addresses, tokens, amounts, references and networks.

1.3 Three kinds of fact

A reserve fact is a fact about custody. It says which addresses bezent controls on which network, what they hold, and every movement in or out of them. Reserve facts are public by construction, because they live on origin networks, and authoritative there.

A supply fact is a fact about the bridged asset on the issuing network. It says how much exists, what was minted, redeemed or destroyed, and against which reference. Supply facts are public and live on the issuing network.

A requester fact is everything else. It says who a funder or beneficiary is, which account a deposit is for, what was quoted or charged, and whether a holder may transact. Requester facts live with the requester. bezent does not hold them or need them.

bezent's own records join the first two kinds: which arrival backs which mint, which redemption funds which payout. They are bezent's working state. They are never the authority on a reserve or supply fact, and every one of them can be checked against the networks (REQ-ST-1).

1.4 Who it is for

  • A requester: a system that asks bezent for deposits and withdrawals on behalf of the people and businesses it serves. The first requester is the operator's own ramp. Others may follow.
  • A ledger that settles on the issuing network: value reaches it and leaves it through a requester. bezent never calls its contract, and it never calls bezent (REQ-LG-*).
  • Anyone who wants to check that a bridged asset is backed.

End users never call bezent. Value they bring in reaches a reserve address through the requester, and value they take out comes from a delivery.

1.5 In scope

  • Arrivals at each bridged asset's reserve, and their observation. Deposit addresses, sweeps, routing between networks, delivery to another network and conversion are the requester's.
  • Backends and their assurance classes.
  • Deposits, minted to the order's destination; withdrawals, from a redemption by anyone; refunds and refund mints; and their lifecycles.
  • One reserve per asset, supply, and the reserve equation.
  • The on-chain component on the issuing network, and its bounds.
  • The surfaces bezent exposes, and its boundary with a ledger on the issuing network, which it never calls.
  • Key custody, governance, incidents and recovery.

1.6 Out of scope

  • Who a funder or beneficiary is, and whether they may transact.
  • Issuing deposit addresses and sweeping them: getting value into a reserve, and from where, is the requester's (§5.2).
  • Prices, quotes and fees charged to anyone, including the price of a conversion (§5.10).
  • Any book of balances: bezent holds no accounts for anyone.
  • Orchestration across third parties: what a requester does with an exchange account or a venue is the requester's business (§5.7).
  • Delivery to a network other than the reserve's: the requester's, from what it receives (REQ-OT-4).
  • Any network that already reaches the issuing network through a trustless native transfer channel. Value between the two moves over that channel, not through a custodian (§13).

1.7 Standing assumptions

  1. One operator, one issuing network. Exactly one operator runs a deployment. It mints on exactly one issuing network.
  2. Trusted custody. The operator is the custodian of every reserve. The issuing network does not verify origin networks, and bezent does not claim that it does (§4.3).
  3. One bridged asset per origin token family, reserved on one backend. Every payout is made on the reserve's network; delivery to any other network is the requester's (REQ-OT-4). Tokens of different issuers are never merged (REQ-AS-3).
  4. Same precision. An origin token is admitted on an origin network only if its precision there equals the bridged asset's (REQ-AS-4).
  5. Successor backing. A bridged asset may later be backed by something other than this bridge, such as an escrow governed by the issuing network. Nothing in this document may prevent that hand-over or require holders to act for it (REQ-AS-6).

Five rules define the architecture.

REQ-AR-1 bezent MUST NOT call a requester to decide an outcome. Every transfer MUST reach a terminal state or a named condition even when every requester is unavailable.

REQ-AR-2 Observers report observations at coordinates. Only the coordinator derives dispositions, and only from observations it recorded. No requester, venue or ledger may assert an outcome to the bridge.

REQ-AR-3 Key material MUST NOT reside in the component that holds bezent's state. The signer MUST sign only a transaction that matches an intent the coordinator recorded durably, and MUST log every signature (INV-9).

REQ-AR-4 Mint and destroy authority over a bridged asset MUST be held only by the on-chain component. That component MUST enforce, by its own code and independently of any off-chain component: single use of every source reference, the supply ceiling, the mint window, the pause, and the roles (INV-2, INV-3, INV-4).

REQ-AR-5 Each invariant in §9 names the layer that enforces it. An implementation MUST NOT satisfy an invariant at a weaker layer than the one named. Any weakening is a specification amendment.

4.3 Trust model — stated plainly

Question Answer
Can the requester make bezent mint value that did not arrive? No. A mint needs a final arrival bezent observed itself, unconsumed (INV-2, INV-6). The requester decides only where it goes.
Can the requester send someone else's deposit to the wrong place? Yes. Where a deposit goes is a requester fact. bezent cannot know who a funder is. A compromised requester can misdirect value. It cannot create it.
Can the operator mint unbacked supply? Yes, up to the bounds. The issuing network does not verify origin networks. The on-chain component caps supply and the mint rate (§7.F, INV-3), and the reserve equation shows any shortfall to anyone who checks (REQ-BK-6).
Can a stolen delivery key drain reserves? Up to the outflow bound of each address it controls, enforced by the network, not by bezent (REQ-KY-3). On the issuing network, up to the mint window.
Can value be lost at a venue? Not reserve. bezent sends no value through a venue. A requester's conversion spends only convertible holdings, which back nothing, and its output lands in the same reserve (REQ-CV-4, REQ-CV-7). V0 uses protocol venues only (DEF-8), and every step of one is observable on chain (REQ-VN-3).
Who is the custodian? The operator, on every origin network. A bridged asset is a claim on the operator's reserves.
Can governance move reserves? Through upgrade, yes. Whoever can upgrade the on-chain component or a reserve address's code can move what it holds. They are named, and an upgrade passes a published process with a delay (REQ-GV-3). On the issuing network, that is the governance of the group that holds the component's authority (REQ-HN-10).
What can anyone check without bezent? Every reserve address and its balance; supply; every mint, redemption and refund mint, with its on-chain reference; for any request reference, the transactions on each network and their disposition (REQ-ST-1).
What can nobody check? That the operator will honour a withdrawal. Custody is trust. The statement of §4.6 makes a breach visible; it does not prevent one.

Trade-off, stated plainly. A trusted bridge is only as good as its custodian and its keys. What it gains is reach and simplicity: any token the operator can hold, on any network it can observe, with no third-party messaging and no runtime changes on other networks. Successor backing (§1.7, assumption 5) is how that trust is reduced later without holders noticing.

4.4 Capability contracts

bezent is built from exactly four external capabilities. Nothing in §5–§8 may require more than these. No requirement in this document names what supplies one.

(a) The issuing network

REQ-HN-1 The issuing network MUST support assets with a fixed precision whose issuance and destruction can be restricted to one account, and that account MUST be able to be an on-chain program (the on-chain component).

REQ-HN-2 It MUST provide a finality signal of its own, and coordinates for every event.

REQ-HN-3 It MUST support transactions that expire: a transaction signed for a bounded window that cannot execute after it, so that non-execution can be proven (REQ-NX-1).

REQ-HN-4 Withdrawn by amendment 0001 (A8).

REQ-HN-5 The on-chain component MUST be able to hold value, keep storage, emit events with references, and refuse a call by its own rules.

REQ-HN-6 Withdrawn by amendment 0001 (A9).

(b) Backends

REQ-RN-1–REQ-RN-6 bind network backends.

REQ-RN-1 An origin network MUST provide a finality signal of its own. A count of confirmations is not one.

REQ-RN-2 It MUST order each sender's transactions by a sequence number (nonce), so that a nonce consumed by a final transaction proves every other transaction with that nonce can never execute.

REQ-RN-3 It MUST emit, for every token movement, an event with a coordinate, the token, the sender, the recipient and the exact amount.

REQ-RN-4 It MUST allow an address whose outflows are restricted by on-chain code, in destination and in amount per window: rules the network enforces, not bezent (REQ-KY-3, REQ-CV-7).

REQ-RN-5 It MUST allow a movement to carry an on-chain reference atomically with the value (REQ-ID-2), by the token itself or by on-chain code that moves the value and emits the reference in one transaction.

REQ-RN-6 Its history over any range bezent has not read MUST be retrievable, so that a gap can be filled (REQ-OB-3).

REQ-BE-1 Each backend MUST implement the backend contract. It MUST observe an arrival with a stable identifier and its finality, deliver idempotently, give evidence of non-execution, and report the reserve it holds.

REQ-BE-2 Each backend MUST declare its assurance class. It is public when every observation, reserve balance and proof of non-execution can be checked from public data. It is attested when they are taken from a named party's reports.

REQ-BE-3 The statement (REQ-PR-1) MUST name each bridged asset's backend and its assurance class.

REQ-BE-4 REQ-BK-6, REQ-NX-2 and REQ-ST-1 bind public backends. For an attested backend, the bridge MUST record each report of the attesting party with that party's own identifiers, and the statement MUST say that the asset's reserve is attested.

REQ-BE-5 For an attested backend whose attesting party is a requester, that requester's report of an arrival, a payout or a reserve balance is the observation. REQ-RQ-7 and REQ-AR-2 ("no caller asserts an outcome") bind public backends only.

Trade-off, stated plainly. For an asset with an attested backend, the attesting requester can cause a mint. What bounds it is the on-chain component's mint window and supply ceiling (§7.F), and the statement, which names the party whose word backs the asset.

5. Domain model

5.1 Assets and routes

REQ-AS-1 A bridged asset MUST have exactly one identity on the issuing network, fixed at creation. The identity MUST NOT encode an origin network or bezent.

REQ-AS-2 An origin token MUST be admitted on an origin network by an explicit, recorded declaration: its network, its token identity, its precision, and the bridged asset it backs. Value of a token not admitted is an unassociated arrival (REQ-IN-6).

REQ-AS-3 One bridged asset MUST be backed only by origin tokens of one issuer. Tokens of different issuers MUST NOT back one bridged asset.

REQ-AS-4 An origin token MUST have the same precision as its bridged asset, or more. Amounts are exact integer minor units everywhere. Where the origin token has more precision, the ratio is a fixed power of ten, declared with the asset (REQ-AS-2).

REQ-AS-9 A deposit of an origin token with more precision mints the arrival divided by the ratio, rounded down. The remainder, smaller than one minor unit of the bridged asset, is recorded in the reserve book as surplus, with the deposit's reference (REQ-BK-4, REQ-BK-7). It is never minted, dropped or returned.

REQ-AS-10 A payout of a bridged asset with less precision than its origin token pays exactly the redeemed amount times the ratio. A payout never creates a remainder.

REQ-AS-5 bezent MUST declare its routes in machine-readable form (REQ-LM-1). A route not declared does not exist.

REQ-AS-7 A convertible token MAY be admitted on an origin network for a bridged asset, by the same kind of recorded declaration as REQ-AS-2, naming the conversion venue route. A convertible token MUST NOT count as reserve, and MUST NOT back any supply.

REQ-AS-6 bezent's mint authority over a bridged asset MUST be revocable by the issuing network's governance, and MUST end when another backing takes over. A hand-over MUST NOT change the bridged asset's identity, and MUST NOT need holders to act. At no point during it may supply exceed what the two backings hold together.

REQ-AS-8 Each bridged asset MUST have exactly one reserve backend. For a network backend, the reserve sits on exactly one network. An attested backend MAY hold it in several positions (accounts), each itemised (REQ-BK-5).

5.3 Reserves, supply and the reserve equation

For a bridged asset A, at a set of final points, one per network:

  • Reserve(A, n) is what bezent's reserve addresses on origin network n hold of A's origin token.
  • InTransit(A) is value moving through a protocol venue: legs out of the reserve that are final, whose destination leg is not.
  • Supply(A) is the total of A on the issuing network.
  • OwedMint(A) is the sum of final arrivals of A's origin tokens not yet minted, whether bound to a deposit or not.
  • OwedPayout(A) is the sum of final redemptions whose payout is not yet final.
  • Surplus(A) is the operator's own value in reserve, recorded per contribution and per cost.

The reserve equation is:

Σ Reserve(A) + InTransit(A) = Supply(A) + OwedMint(A) + OwedPayout(A) + Surplus(A)

REQ-BK-1 bezent MUST compute the reserve equation for every bridged asset, from chain data at final points, continuously (NFR-5). Any divergence, to the smallest unit, MUST pause the asset's routes and page a human (REQ-IC-2).

REQ-BK-2 InTransit, OwedMint and OwedPayout MUST each be itemised: a list of the transfers and venue deliveries that make it up, never a residual.

REQ-BK-3 Surplus MUST be zero or more at all times. A negative surplus is a shortfall of backing, handled as a divergence.

REQ-BK-10 The operator MUST keep each asset's surplus above a floor it declares, and an alert MUST fire before the floor is reached. The operator tops up the surplus from its own funds.

REQ-BK-4 Every network cost, every venue fee and every amount difference on a venue delivery MUST be recorded against surplus, with its reference. None may reduce a delivery.

REQ-BK-5 Every address that holds reserve or a fee position MUST be published per network and token. Value MUST move in or out of a reserve address only by a transfer, a conversion under REQ-CV-7, or a recorded surplus contribution or withdrawal, each with a reference.

REQ-BK-6 For a public backend, anyone holding the published addresses and a statement MUST be able to recompute both sides of the equation from public data (REQ-BE-4).

REQ-BK-7 bezent MUST keep a reserve book per bridged asset. It is a double-entry, append-only record of every movement into, out of and between the asset's reserve positions, value in transit, owed mints, owed payouts and surplus, and of supply as the issuing network reports it. A correction is a new entry; no entry is ever changed or deleted (INV-17).

REQ-BK-8 The reserve book is working state, never the authority. Each evaluation of the reserve equation (REQ-BK-1) MUST re-derive every term from the backends and the issuing network at final points, and compare it with the book. Any difference is a divergence.

REQ-BK-9 The reserve book need not be provable to third parties. The statement (REQ-PR-1) MUST say so, and MUST say, per asset, which terms anyone can recompute from public data and which rest on an attesting party's reports (REQ-BE-4).

5.5 Deposits (origin network → issuing network)

REQ-IN-1 An arrival MUST be observed at a coordinate and recorded once, de-duplicated by coordinate (INV-10).

REQ-IN-2 An arrival counts as final only when the network's final point has passed its block and the block at that height still has the hash observed (REQ-OB-2).

REQ-IN-3 A final arrival of an admitted token at a reserve address MUST wait as an unassociated arrival (CND-Unassociated) until a requester binds it with a deposit request that names its coordinate (REQ-RQ-3). The deposit then begins at Source-Final, with a bridge reference derived deterministically from the coordinate.

REQ-IN-4 A deposit MUST be minted only against one final, unconsumed arrival of the same token family and the same amount, and the mint MUST carry that arrival's on-chain reference (INV-2, INV-6).

REQ-IN-5 A deposit's destination is an address on the issuing network. Delivering into a ledger is the requester's, from its own account.

REQ-IN-6 An arrival that cannot become a deposit (a token not admitted, an amount outside the route's bounds, value with no matching deposit request, venue delivery or surplus contribution) MUST be recorded as an unassociated arrival with an owner (CND-Unassociated). An arrival with no deposit request by the route's deadline stays CND-Unassociated with its owner. It MUST NOT be absorbed into reserve, minted or dropped. Releasing it is a refund request by a requester with the destination it supplies, or a recorded surplus contribution by the operator, each with a second approver.

REQ-IN-7 An observation that stops matching the chain before finality MUST be withdrawn, reported as withdrawn, and anything waiting on it MUST stop.

REQ-IN-8 A deposit whose mint the issuing network refuses holds CND-DestinationRefused, with nothing minted (REQ-IN-9).

REQ-IN-9 bezent MUST NOT check whether a destination on the issuing network can receive the asset. A mint the network refuses because of its destination holds CND-DestinationRefused and mints nothing. Past the route's deadline it goes to Needs-Review.

5.6 Withdrawals (issuing network → origin network)

REQ-OT-1 A withdrawal is funded by a redemption on the issuing network, and only by one.

REQ-OT-2 A withdrawal MUST be bound to its redemption by the redemption's on-chain reference: the redeemer's account together with the reference it chose. It MUST NOT be matched by amount or time; the redeemer is part of the identifier, not a matching rule.

REQ-OT-3 bezent MUST NOT submit a payout before the redemption is final.

REQ-OT-4 A withdrawal MUST be paid from the asset's reserve, on the reserve's network, to the beneficiary the redemption names. Delivery to another network is the requester's, from what it receives.

REQ-OT-5 When the asset's reserve cannot cover a withdrawal, the withdrawal MUST hold CND-ReserveShort, raise one condition per (asset, network) to the requesters, and resume by itself when reserve recovers. It MUST NOT fail, refund or move to another network.

REQ-OT-6 The redeemed value is destroyed at redemption. If every payout attempt is proven not executed at finality, the redeemer is refunded by one refund mint tied to that redemption.

REQ-OT-7 and REQ-OT-8 Withdrawn by amendment 0001 (A6).

Trade-off, stated plainly. Burning at once means a failed payout is made whole by the mint authority. A refund mint is bounded by the mint window and is one per redemption, so a stolen delivery key cannot use refunds to exceed the window.

5.8 Refunds

REQ-RF-1 A refund MUST happen only when the leg it replaces is proven not executed and the proof is final. Never on a timer, never on an unknown.

REQ-RF-2 The refund destination of a deposit MUST be given in its request. bezent MUST NOT choose it afterwards. A withdrawal's refund destination is always its redeemer (REQ-RF-4).

REQ-RF-3 A refund MUST be a transfer of its own, with a bridge reference derived deterministically from the original's, under every requirement here. The original's record MUST point to it.

REQ-RF-4 A withdrawal's refund is a refund mint to the redeemer, tied to the redemption's reference. It counts against the mint window.

REQ-RF-5 After a proof of non-execution, bezent MAY submit a new destination attempt for the same withdrawal instead of refunding, until the withdrawal's deadline. Each attempt MUST be recorded and proven before the next.

5.9 Review

REQ-RV-1 A transfer or venue delivery with an unknown leg past its deadline MUST go to Needs-Review, and so MUST a transfer whose delivery attempt's preparation was refused without a named condition past the route's deadline (REQ-EO-3), and one for which a delivery anomaly was recorded, which makes its leg unknown at once (REQ-NX-3), with no deadline to wait for. Each goes with an owner and a service level, and MUST NOT be refunded, retried as a new transaction, or failed. The owner of Needs-Review and CND-Unassociated is the operator's operations function, and the service level is one business day.

REQ-RV-2 A review MUST end only by evidence: a coordinate that settles the disposition, which the bridge verifies itself, after which the ordinary steps run. A resolution that moves value MUST have a second approver (REQ-GV-5).

5.10 Conversions

REQ-CV-1 An arrival of a convertible token MUST NOT be minted against. It is held in CND-AwaitingConversion until a conversion turns it into the origin token.

REQ-CV-2 A conversion on a deposit MUST be executed by the requester under REQ-CV-7, as one transaction whose output lands in the reserve. The deposit is then minted against that output, at its observed amount, under every rule of §5.5. The deposit records the convertible arrival and the converted output, with both amounts.

REQ-CV-3 Withdrawn by amendment 0007 (A2), with AC-B4.1 and AC-B4.2: every payout is in the origin token; a requester that wants another token converts what it receives.

REQ-CV-4 Until converted, convertible holdings MUST be itemised per token and network, outside the reserve equation, and published in the statement (REQ-PR-1). They back nothing.

REQ-CV-5 The price, the slippage and any fee of a conversion are the requester's. bezent MUST NOT make a delivery or a mint up to a quoted amount; it mints or delivers what was observed.

REQ-CV-6 A conversion that does not complete by its deadline goes to Needs-Review (REQ-RV-1). Its convertible holdings stay itemised; they are never written off and never counted as reserve.

REQ-CV-7 A requester MAY hold a role on a reserve address that lets it convert, at a protocol venue, convertible holdings at that address into the origin token. The network MUST enforce, by on-chain code (REQ-RN-4), that the role (a) spends only convertible tokens admitted for that asset, never the origin token, (b) sends the output only to the same reserve address, and (c) is bounded per window. The role MUST NOT move value out of the reserve address by any other path. Every conversion is observed at its coordinate and recorded like any other movement.

The requester converts, but it never holds a key that can take reserve out, never asserts an arrival, and never stands in for the reserve equation. Convertible holdings back nothing until converted (REQ-CV-4), so the role puts no reserve at risk.

The trade-off on conversions through a custodial venue is withdrawn by amendment 0001 (A10): V0 uses protocol venues only (DEF-8).

8. Normative behaviour

8.1 Observation and finality

REQ-OB-1 bezent MUST publish, per network, the finality rule it applies. It MUST be the network's own finality signal (REQ-HN-2, REQ-RN-1), never a count of confirmations.

REQ-OB-2 A leg MUST count as final only when the network's final point has passed its block, and the block at that height still has the hash observed.

REQ-OB-3 Observation of each network MUST be complete over contiguous ranges of blocks. bezent MUST NOT move past a range it failed to read; it MUST record that range as a visible gap and fill it from history (REQ-RN-6).

REQ-OB-4 Until final, an observation MUST be re-checked every cycle (REQ-IN-7).

REQ-OB-5 Every read that decides a disposition MUST be made at a final block. Before declaring a source leg unknown, bezent SHOULD look for its destination: a final destination leg proves the source executed.

REQ-OB-6 bezent MUST check the execution result of every transaction it observes, not only its inclusion.

REQ-OB-7 A mint MUST rest on the backend's own finality signal. For an attested backend, that is the party's report that the payment is final under its rail's rules. The party MUST NOT report a payment final before its rail guarantees it.

8.2 Non-execution

REQ-NX-1 Every transaction bezent signs MUST make non-execution provable in bounded time. On the issuing network: an expiring transaction whose window has ended at a final block, with the signer's nonce not consumed by it. On an origin network: the signer's nonce consumed at a final block by a different transaction. An on-chain refusal counts once it is final.

REQ-NX-2 A proof of non-execution MUST be checkable by anyone from public chain data alone: network, signer, nonce, expiry where there is one, and the final block that proves it.

REQ-NX-3 Silence, elapsed time, a node's "not found", a connection error, an event bezent cannot decode, or a transaction dropped from a pool MUST NOT count as proof. Each is unknown.

REQ-NX-4 On an origin network, where transactions do not expire, bezent MUST be able to force non-execution by consuming the nonce with a different transaction that moves no value, and SHOULD do so when a delivery is past its deadline and not included.

8.3 References and exactly once

REQ-ID-1 A repeat with the same request reference and the same body MUST return the original's status. A repeat with a different body MUST be refused (ERR-ReferenceConflict). Neither starts a second transfer.

REQ-ID-2 The on-chain reference MUST be written on chain by the transactions of each movement, on every network it touches, atomically with the value (REQ-RN-5). Legs MUST be matched to transfers by it, never by position in a batch or by a value only one direction can compute. References of different kinds (a mint's, a redemption's) are separate namespaces on the issuing network; one never consumes another.

REQ-ID-3 A bridge reference MUST be distinct from the request reference and MUST NOT replace it.

REQ-ID-4 Knowing a reference MUST NOT be enough to change a transfer. Correlating is not authorising.

REQ-EO-1 bezent MUST persist each exact signed transaction before submitting it. After a restart it MUST re-send those bytes or query their status, never sign a second transaction with the same intent until the first is proven not executed.

REQ-EO-2 Each transfer MUST be minted or delivered at most once, forever: across retries, restarts, key rotation, fail-over, upgrades, and the loss of bezent's own database (INV-2).

REQ-EO-3 Every final source leg MUST eventually be delivered, refunded, or held in a named condition or Needs-Review with an owner.

REQ-EO-4 bezent MUST declare which transfers share a signing lane (one sender's nonce sequence) and whether order is kept within one. One unknown transfer SHOULD NOT hold up unrelated transfers.

REQ-EO-5 bezent MAY group several transfers into one transaction, provided each keeps its own on-chain reference, its own recorded outcome and its own proof of non-execution.

REQ-EO-6 A new attempt MUST carry the same on-chain reference, and MUST NOT be signed before the previous attempt is proven not executed at finality.

8.4 Limits and backpressure

REQ-LM-1 bezent MUST declare, per route, in machine-readable form: minimum and maximum per transfer, window limits, cut-offs, current capacity, any minimum a venue imposes, the venue addresses for venue routes, and the route's completion budget. A route's minimum is at least one minor unit of the bridged asset, expressed in the origin token. A transfer whose next step falls outside its route's declared cut-off holds CND-CutOff until the route reopens, then resumes by itself. No request is refused for a cut-off.

REQ-LM-2 bezent MUST refuse at request time, with a named error and before any value moves, anything it can already tell will not execute.

REQ-LM-3 After acceptance, a shortfall in reserve, in a fee position or in the mint window MUST be a named, parked condition that resumes by itself, with one alert per position. It MUST NOT be a failure or start a refund.

REQ-LM-4 When bezent cannot keep up, it MUST refuse new requests with ERR-Overloaded rather than accept work it will not finish within budget.

8.5 Status

REQ-ST-1 Given only a request reference, anyone MUST be able to find the transfer's transactions on each network, and check its disposition from public chain data, without bezent's database.

REQ-ST-2 bezent MUST answer a status query by request reference, bridge reference and coordinate.

REQ-ST-3 Every report MUST carry observations, not verdicts: a coordinate on each network bezent touched, the network's own data and error text, and finality as observed. Accepting a request is not an observation of anything.

REQ-ST-6 The following MUST be observable continuously: the age of the oldest non-final transfer per route; the count and age of unknown legs; observation lag and open gaps per network; the reserve equation per asset; reserve per network; fee positions; undelivered notifications; arrivals in CND-Unassociated, with their age.

REQ-ST-7 For any transfer, bezent MUST be able to state from durable state alone, after any restart: what it was asked, what each network did, and what it told the requester.

8.6 Keys and governance

REQ-KY-1 Signing keys MUST be held as REQ-CU-1 says. Key material MUST NOT be readable from a process environment, and MUST NOT appear in any configuration, log, URL or error.

REQ-KY-2 Withdrawn by amendment 0007 (A1).

REQ-KY-3 Every key that can move reserve or mint MUST be bounded on chain, by an outflow bound per asset and window that the network enforces.

REQ-KY-4 Keys MUST be separate per network and per role (delivery, pause, governance), and rotatable without stranding transfers in flight.

REQ-KY-5 Deliveries above a stated size, and every administrative action, SHOULD need several independent signers.

REQ-KY-6 A key or account from a test environment MUST NOT hold any authority over production addresses or components, and production keys MUST NOT sign on test networks.

REQ-GV-1 bezent MUST be pausable per route, per asset, per direction and globally. A pause MUST stop new work only. Transfers in flight MUST keep being observed and completed, never failed or refunded because of the pause.

REQ-GV-2 Pausing SHOULD need one authorised party. Unpausing and raising a bound SHOULD need several.

REQ-GV-3 Whoever can upgrade on-chain code that mints, destroys or holds reserve MUST be named, and upgrades MUST pass a published governance process with a delay before taking effect.

REQ-GV-4 Upgrading off-chain components MUST NOT lose or duplicate transfers in flight.

REQ-GV-5 Every administrative or manual action MUST be recorded with its actor and justification in an append-only record. There MUST be no path that moves value or edits a status outside the ordinary steps: a manual resolution attaches evidence (a coordinate) and runs them, and one that moves value needs a second approver.

8.7 Incidents

REQ-IC-1 At start-up, bezent MUST check that each network it reaches is the one declared, by its genesis or chain identity, and fail closed on a mismatch.

REQ-IC-2 On a condition it cannot explain (a divergence in the reserve equation, a finality stall, an observation gap, a reorganisation deeper than expected, a signature it did not record), bezent MUST pause the affected routes, keep observing, and page a human.

REQ-IC-3 During an incident, transfers in flight MUST be reported as unknown or delayed, never as failed, and MUST NOT be refunded.

REQ-IC-4 A signature bezent did not record MUST be treated as a key compromise: pause, rotate, report.

REQ-IC-6 Each incident class in notes/BRIDGE-REQUIREMENTS.md §5, and each named condition here, SHOULD have a runbook exercised before bezent carries value (NFR-9).

13. Non-goals

  • bezent is not an exchange. It never prices or executes a conversion. The requester converts at a protocol venue, inside the reserve (REQ-CV-7), and bezent mints only what the conversion delivered (§5.10).
  • bezent is not a ledger. It holds no balances for people. It holds reserves, and it mints and destroys supply.
  • bezent is not a workflow engine. Requesters orchestrate; bezent executes requests and reports observations.
  • bezent is not trustless. It states its trust and makes it checkable. Reducing it is successor backing (§1.7), not a feature of this specification.
  • bezent does not decide who may transact. It holds no personal data and checks no eligibility.
  • bezent does not compete with a trustless channel. A network that already exchanges value with the issuing network through a native, trustless transfer channel is never an origin network. Putting a custodian on a path that needs none would only add trust.
